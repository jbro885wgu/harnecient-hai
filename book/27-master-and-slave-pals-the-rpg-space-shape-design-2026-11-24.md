## 1. The model (DESIGN)
- **Two pals first: one master, one slave.** More later; the model must not break at 2, 3 or 6 (the house party is 6).
- **The master is the FIRST pal of the party** ("the first pal in the pokeballs"). It is **the interface**: the one the human talks to. It **does not fight** (it is not one of the slaves; in the house a battle uses the first living actor, so "never fights" is a rule row for the master, not an accident). It **controls the other pets**.
- **The master is the user's hands and will.** The human gives intent in words; the master turns it into verbs on the slaves (and on itself where it may). It acts "almost as if the user does not exist": it carries on with the user's standing wishes without being told each step, and the human steers by talking to it, not by driving slaves by hand. Every action it takes is a ledger row with the actor `master` (so what it did on the user's behalf can be read, graded and undone), and its permissions are the settings of book 25 section 4 (hard rules, hand-tuned biases, permission growth).
- **The slave** does the body work (walk, fetch, plant, carry ...). It takes orders from the master, may be taught words by the master, and is graded like any hai agent.
- **The pals chat with each other.** Pal-to-pal speech is a ledger row `SAY|from|to|text`; each pal answers from its own bank (each is a hai agent folder, so it answers with its own corpus and trust weights and leaves a WHY row). The house already has a pet-to-pet channel (see section 2), shipped OFF; hai-pal's own channel is rows in its ledger so it is replayable and visible in the Viz tab.
- **Relation to book 25 section 7 (the main agent).** Book 25 said hai-cli is the entity in slot 1, **not embodied**, with the application-level memory. The owner's master is a pal of the party. **OPEN (owner):** are they the same entity (the master has a body on the map but never fights) or two roles (hai-cli = the app-level memory agent with no body; the master = party slot 1 with a body)? **Default I will build:** the master has a body, never fights, and holds the global memory rows (book 25 section 7); a user who prefers a CLI-only experience still gets the master as a text interface with no map.

## 3. What "acceptable" means for hai-pal in rpg-edit (the checklist the owner asked for; DESIGN, each item needs a check that can fail and a frame opened by eye)
1. **Pals on the left sidebar instead of palettes** when the hai-pal book is open, in edit and in play, with the master first and marked as master. (Data first: the book's `layouts.pdl`; the list comes from the book's own pals, not the editor's four.)
2. **Master and one slave on the map** as figures at the cells the ledger says, in their colours; the master never walks into a fight (there is none yet) and is shown first in the list.
3. **Talk to the master** in the right chat bar (the editor has one) and see it answer from its own bank with the WHY line; **the master orders the slave** and the slave's figure moves (ledger rows by actor `master`).
4. **The pals speak to each other**, visible as rows and in the chat, switchable off (a rate of 0 or an ENABLED row).
5. **Play starts the agents' loop** (a `GAME | driver` row), Stop pauses it, and a Play press never changes the user's saved book.
6. **The book is conformant** (`book_check` A1 to A10 and B1 to B4 pass) so it is a citizen of the page file, not a skin.
7. **Every rule is an op behind an event page** (blueprintz can show it); the world rows are the house EVENT shape (owner choice 2026-11-24).
8. **Both entry points** (standalone `hai-cli` and the book) open the same session and give the same world; one case replays one ledger through both.
9. **A real frame of the live window for each of 1 to 5**, opened and described, with the user's saved state restored by hash afterwards.
Until these pass, hai-pal is a **first slice** (book 26 section 10 and the house doc section 10), not "acceptable". I will say which items pass when I report.

## 5. CORRECTION and additions (owner, 2026-11-24, two more messages): the role is the ORDER, and the master has meta control
Owner: *"its supposed to be about rearrange/order, remember?"* and *"the master has meta control of both the interface and the other bots."* Sections 1 and 3 above treated "master" as a pal; this section replaces that reading where they differ.
- **Order is the role (DESIGN, replaces the OPEN question of book 25 section 7).** The party is an ORDERED list. **Whoever is first is the master; everyone else is a slave.** The role belongs to the SLOT, not to the name: put another pal first and it becomes the master, the old master becomes a slave. The master's global-memory rows (`MAIN|...`, book 25 section 7) follow the slot too (default; the owner confirms). This is why the party order matters and why reordering is not a side feature.
- **Reordering is therefore NOT held any more** (book 25 section 8 held it). Verb `REORDER|actor|pal|to_slot` as a ledger row (the actor may be the user, or the master only if its permissions allow). The user interface for it stays the owner's idea from 2026-11-23: a move mode on the focused row; my recommendation is still `m` to enter it (marker `↕`), then `^` and `v` (or the arrows) to move the row, Enter or Esc to leave it, because `^` already means the In mode in the shared nav. **NOT FOUND in the house:** searched `rpg_edit_hq.c`, `petslike_core.c`, `party.pdl`, `verbs.pdl` for swap, reorder, leader, first pal: no party-order verb exists, so this is new work and the first pal of the existing party is only first by file order.
- **Meta control of the interface (DESIGN).** The master can operate the application the way the user does: open and close the side bars (including the left pals bar), switch book and page, set the view, and show what the user asks to see. Each is a verb with a ledger row and a permission, calling the same ops the human's clicks already call (the house has `ui_mode_op` for bars, `pal_side` for the left slot, `edit_last.sh` for book and page; the relay keys and mouse events are what a person's input becomes). Nothing here may bypass the verb funnel: no hidden state, no "the master moved a window without a row".
- **Meta control of the other bots (DESIGN).** The master orders the slaves (move, fetch, plant, speak, teach them a word, grade them, teleport them between pages) through the same verbs a user would use on them; the slaves answer to nobody else by default. The master itself does not fight.
- **Meta means above, with limits.** The user stays above the master: hard rules and biases (book 25 section 4) can forbid any interface or bot verb for the master, and permission grows only by the user's rule. Every action carries the actor `master`, so what it did "as the user's hands and will" is readable, gradable and reversible. A refusal is a row.
- **Acceptance checklist, added items (section 3 continues):** 10. putting another pal first (by the move mode) changes who answers the user's talk and who may order the others, and the left pals bar shows the new order; 11. the master opens or closes the left pals bar and switches page on a spoken request, and each is a row with actor `master`; 12. a hard rule that forbids a master interface verb produces a REFUSED row and no change on screen; 13. after a reorder the old master cannot order the new one. Each needs a case that can fail and a frame opened by eye.
- **Order of work (replaces section 4 (a) and (b)):** (a) the party as an ordered list in the book's data and the `REORDER` verb with its case (the whole role model hangs on it); (b) the left pals bar showing that order, as data if the board reads the book's layout, otherwise by the smallest generic change; (c) the master and slave roles from the order; (d) the master's interface verbs and bot verbs behind permissions; (e) the world rows in the house EVENT shape, event pages, the Play driver, conformance, the same session in both entry points; (f) the web pet and the video.

## 7. READ 2026-11-24: where the left pals bar gets its list (answers two of the three NOT VERIFIED facts of section 2)
- The pets bar (`side-l`, `board/pchq-board.xhtpm` lines 115-124) is a `<repeat bind="party">` over `n_party` and `party_<i>_label/_id/_cls` that `petslike_core.c` writes into the window's `ui.txt` (line ~1002). The list is built by `load_party()` (line 486): the names are the **RPG Maker DB** `SYSTEM|name|PetParty` group `member1..6` of the APP's own `db/system.pdl` (else the shared `&.widgits/db-hq/data`, else the `fallback_party` config), each filled in from `actors.pdl`, then the app's `pals.pdl` rows appended, 17 entries in the live editor. **So the party ORDER is the DB member order; the pokeballs are `member1..6`, and member1 is the first pal.** (This is where REORDER must land for the real editor: it rewrites that order, or the book's own copy of it.)
- The party is chosen **per APP (the process), not per open book**: nothing in `rpg-edit-hq` (button.sh, ops, pdl) sets `PET_PARTY` or `PET_DB` from the book that is open (searched). Opening the `hai-pal` book therefore still shows the editor's own party; a book supplies a party only as its OWN petslike app (as `rpg-space` does with its `party.pdl` and `db`).
- **Consequence (DESIGN, not built):** pals on the left for hai-pal needs either (a) hai-pal as its own petslike app with a `db/system.pdl` (PetParty master, slave), `actors.pdl` and a `GAME | driver` row (which the plan needs anyway: Play), then check what the bar shows when that app's Play runs; or (b) a generic change that lets the editor read the open book's party. (a) is the house shape and touches no shared code; (b) is shared code. Recommended: (a) first. NOT VERIFIED: that the editor's bar follows a book's own driver at all.
